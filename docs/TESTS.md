# Tests

How the Renew test files are grouped. Run them with:

```bash
npm test
python3 tests/browser_check.py http://127.0.0.1:8080/playground/ path/to/axe.min.js
```

1,478 checks pass in total, as of v1.9.0. GitHub Actions runs `npm test` on every push before the playground is published. The suite also runs `python3 tests/verify/independent.py --check`, which regenerates the independent Python fixtures in memory and fails if they no longer match `tests/fixtures/independent.json`.

Core language
- `test_math.mjs` (48 checks): the payment formula against closed forms, five rate and frequency mixes, zero rates, accelerated payments, extra and lump sums, the solvers, and formatting. Includes the classic $100,000 at 5% over 25 years, $581.60 a month.
- `test_language.mjs` (120 checks): values and units, scenario inheritance, comments and line endings, checks, posted rate lookup, the six day posted rate cutoff (November 24, 2026 reads the November 18 week, November 25 is an error, and docs/LANGUAGE.md, docs/INTEGRATION.md, the README and DEVPOST.md state the same rule word for word, with none of the older wording left), and 25 error cases with their line, column and hint. Round 10: two labels on one rate line are refused at the second, with its line and column, whether they sit around a path or side by side, and the grammar has one label slot; `renews at` in `sweep` and `break even`, long and short form, gives the same table and answers as `renews`.

Comparing plans
- `test_horizons_and_sweeps.mjs` (68 checks): equal horizons and the different term warning, `renews at`, sweeps and their type errors, the `variable` label and the rule that it needs a rate path, `renews at` or a sweep of `rate` or `renews`, payoff assumptions, and runtime errors that must read the same from the interpreter and from compiled code.
- `test_fair_comparisons.mjs` (119 checks): the payment after renewal in sweeps and comparisons, `against`, the posted against typed note, the balance note, variable rate warnings, percentage points, `paid`, a bare `variable` refused at the word with its hint, and refused too when the only thing beside it is `payment held` or a sweep of `balance`, `extra` or `term`, while a sweep of `renews` or a rate path with `payment held` runs, a bare `remaining` refused with a hint, and the 2026 example. Round 10: a `renews at`, a rate path, a held path or a renewal path whose every rate equals the starting rate is refused at the word `variable`, also when the rate comes from an input (and runs once the input moves it), with the same message and line from compiled code; a renewal path that moves later, a renews at one hundredth of a point away, or a rate sweep beside a same rate renews at still runs.

Rates that change
- `test_paths_break_even_inputs.mjs` (108 checks): rate paths inside the term and renewal paths, with their payments, interest, balances and explain lines; path errors; break even for the 2026 example; `amortization to fit` and `remaining from`; continuation lines; `input` overrides; compiled `run(inputs)` and `compile --module`; and `npm run rates` against a saved sample of the Valet download.
- `test_payment_held.mjs` (59 checks): `payment held` against an adjustable payment on the same path, `payment held` with no rate change refused at the word `payment` (also with only an extra sweep), and a held plan with a rate sweep matching the plain plan row for row, the trigger rate formula, the error when a path reaches it, renewal after a held payment term, scenarios, the explain lines, and a trigger-rate boundary: just under the later trigger still runs, one step over is refused. Just under the trigger the time to pay off is the real one, 106 years 1 month, with the note that it is a mathematical projection, not a contract term; a $1 billion loan held just under its trigger still pays off (318 years 8 months) inside the 1,000 year limit; and a plan that reaches the limit is reported as not repaid, not as paid off.

Answers and inputs
- `test_break_even.mjs` (72 checks): break even on every setting, each answer checked by working out the plan at the grid value either side without the search; the grid wording; the note for `by paid`; no answer in the range; the errors; and a non-monotonic case where `by paid` against amortization crosses twice, so Renew reports several crossings and no single value. What the scan guarantees: made-up answers that change twice three grid steps apart, once one step wide, and four times close together are all found for rates (the old 400 sample scan would have missed the first); every month is tried for lengths of time; for money, the note says every cent is covered if the bound in docs/LANGUAGE.md holds. A brute force over all 30,001 renewal rates for the 2026 example, and month by month for the amortization case, agrees with the search. Stopping each plan at the horizon during the search is checked against the whole plan on over 1,500 plans and horizons. The short form `break even SETTING "A" vs "B"` gives the same report as the long form, interpreted and compiled, and `examples/07-two-crossings.renew` finds both pairs of changes.
- `test_certified_money.mjs` (75 checks): the certified money search and its bound. The combined band is checked against the real measure on 5,000 random pairs of values (seed 20261119) with none broken. Round 9 checks each half of the bound on its own: an exact copy of the kernel with rounding switched off never moves more than `K` per dollar, and the real kernel is never more than `R` from that exact copy, on over 5,000 random amounts (seed 20261120); one cent of balance that moves the payment by a cent moves everything paid by about `N` cents, and `2R` covers it; `R` matches the formula in docs/LANGUAGE.md. Five cases, one for extra interest, accelerated biweekly, weekly paid, a lump window and a balance window, are brute forced over every cent of a window and must match the search. An adversarial case has two changes 29 cents apart, at $15,997.68 and $15,997.97; the old scan, $500 apart, finds neither, and the certified search finds both. A plan paid off inside the horizon stays certified. When the budget runs out the search falls back to the sampled scan, and the note says so and why. Round 10: fallback case 2 is triggered for real on a copy of the kernel with the older payment rule (a $1.00 balance at 2% over 25 years rounded to a $0.00 payment), with its reason in the note, while the real kernel stays certified on the same search; every balance from $1.00 to $1,000.00 on four plans (2%, 8%, 20% over 50 years, and 30% over 50 years weekly) stops under the old rule and never under the new one; 2,000 ordinary plans keep exactly the same payment. `amortization to fit` with a balance search: the amortization is fitted once and held, the search stays certified, matches the plan with that amortization written out and a walk over every cent within $30, claim (a) holds on 400 random pairs, and the notes say the amortization is held. 30 made up answers built to use all of `K` and all of `R` (seed 20261122), with dips just under, exactly at and just over the target and two dips one cent apart, must give exactly the changes a walk over every cent gives. 24 random plan pairs (seed 20261123): every cent within $25 of each reported change is walked and agrees. No file says every cent is covered without saying it depends on the bound.
- `test_inputs.mjs` (20 checks): the one input rule in `run()`, compiled code, the ES module, the command line and the playground.
- `test_json_and_embed.mjs` (42 checks): `renew run --json`, `value` and `type`, `raw` and `units` for every kind of table, errors with `--json`, and that the embed module is the current `compile --module` output and gives 4.889% and 5.388%. The documented `run(inputs)` contract in docs/INTEGRATION.md: the `inputs` shape, `{ blocks, report }`, each error message quoted word for word, a bare number refused, `examples/minimal.html`, and the README `<script type="module">` snippet run as written.

Independent figures and agreement
- `test_independent.mjs` (131 checks): 13 cases worked out by `tests/verify/independent.py`, a separate Python calculation with decimal arithmetic that shares no code with Renew, must match the interpreter and the compiled code to the cent. The cases cover several rate changes, extra and lump payments, accelerated and weekly payments, monthly compounding, short horizons and five `payment held` cases, one held just under the later trigger, where the payoff runs past 100 years. It found one real difference, accelerated payments at a rate change rounding in a different order from the start, which is now fixed.
- `test_compiler.mjs` (610 checks): 7 examples and 300 random programs, compiled and interpreted, must give the same report byte for byte and the same result blocks, plus 3 checks that compiled code has no `require`, folds posted rates in as cited constants, and comments each source line. Each random program uses every statement and measure, both solvers, posted and typed rates, all compounding choices and payment frequencies, `extra`, `lump`, `renews at`, rate and renewal paths, `payment held`, `amortization to fit` and `remaining from`, an `input`, and continued lines. Every tenth random program also has `break even renews` twice and `break even` on a random setting; the rest leave them out, because each break even now works out about 30,000 plans.
- `test_cli.mjs` (6 checks): run, check, compile and exit codes.
- `browser_check.py`: the "Scenarios, not contract terms" box, every example (7) at 1440 and 390 pixels, the 2026 example's notes and sweep, the comparison table fitting beside the editor, the break even line, the input box rerunning the program without changing its text and refusing a value without a percent sign, the `payment held` example, the trigger-rate boundary example, the embed page and the minimal page, each with its input and its error at 1100 and 390 pixels, stacked cards with no table scrolling sideways at 390 pixels, variable rate warning boxes, compiled output run in the page matching the interpreter, the error state, keyboard tab switching, no sideways scroll, and axe-core with WCAG 2.2 AA rules in 36 page states. An automated check, not a full accessibility audit.
